# UNC311: Probability and Statistics
**Semester**: Semester IV | **Program**: B.E. Electronics and Computer Engineering (ENC)
**Category**: BSC | **L-T-P**: 3-1-0 | **Credits**: 3.5 | **Contact Hours**: 4

## 🎯 Course Objective
To introduce engineering students to fundamentals of probability and statistics required for communication systems, modelling of random variables and processes, noise modelling, autocorrelation, and source coding.

## 📋 Course Learning Objectives (CLO) / Course Outcomes (CO)
Upon successful completion of this course, the students will be able to:
- **CLO1**: Analyze and compute statistical measures to describe and interpret data characteristics and distribution behaviour across applications.
- **CLO2**: Apply probability and random variable concepts to model and analyze discrete and continuous probability distributions.
- **CLO3**: Analyze random processes and noise models by evaluating stationarity, ergodicity, correlation functions, and power spectral density.
- **CLO4**: Evaluate estimation and hypothesis testing methods by assessing bias, variance, mean square error (MSE), confidence intervals, and error probabilities for engineering problems.
- **CLO5**: Analyze information-theoretic models and coding schemes by computing entropy, mutual information, and channel capacity, and applying source coding theorems to improve transmission efficiency and reliability.

## 📚 Syllabus & Topics
- Unit 1: Statistics: Measures of central tendency, dispersion, moments, skewness, kurtosis.
- Unit 2: Probability & Random Variables: Sample space, axioms, Bayes theorem. Discrete and continuous distributions (Bernoulli, Binomial, Poisson, Uniform, Gaussian), Joint distributions, Central Limit Theorem.
- Unit 3: Random Processes & Noise Modelling: Strict-sense and wide-sense stationarity, Ergodicity, Autocorrelation, Power Spectral Density, Wiener-Khinchin theorem, AWGN model.
- Unit 4: Estimation & Hypothesis Testing: Parameter estimation, MSE, Confidence intervals, Hypothesis testing, Type-I and Type-II errors, Neyman-Pearson lemma.
- Unit 5: Information Theory & Coding: Entropy, Mutual information, Shannon's source coding theorem, Huffman coding, Channel capacity (BSC, BEC, AWGN).
