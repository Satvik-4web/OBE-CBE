# UES013: Electrical & Electronics Engineering
**Semester**: Semester II | **Program**: B.E. Electronics and Computer Engineering (ENC)
**Category**: ESC | **L-T-P**: 3-1-2 | **Credits**: 4.5 | **Contact Hours**: 6

## 🎯 Course Objective
To introduce the basic concepts of electrical and electronics engineering.

## 📋 Course Learning Objectives (CLO) / Course Outcomes (CO)
Upon successful completion of this course, the students will be able to:
- **CLO1**: Apply various networks laws and theorems to solve dc circuits.
- **CLO2**: Compute different ac quantities with phasor representation.
- **CLO3**: Comprehend the operation in magnetic circuits, single phase transformer.
- **CLO4**: Recognize and apply number systems and simplification of Boolean expressions using Boolean algebra and K-Map.
- **CLO5**: Solve and implement logical functions using logic gates, decoders, and multiplexers.
- **CLO6**: Discuss and explain the working of Flip-Flops, diode, transistor and operational amplifier, their configurations and applications.

## 📚 Syllabus & Topics
- DC Circuits: Introduction to circuit elements; independent and dependent sources; Kirchhoff's laws; mesh and node analysis; network theorems: Superposition, Thevenin's, Norton's, Maximum power transfer theorem; star-delta transformation; transient response of R-L and R-C circuits.
- AC Circuits & Magnetic Circuits: Phasor representation of circuit elements; series and parallel AC circuits; resonance in RLC circuits; balanced three phase circuits; three phase power measurement using two-wattmeter method; magnetic circuits, single-phase transformer.
- Digital Logic Design: Number systems, Boolean Algebra, Karnaugh Maps, Logic gates, Adder, Decoder, MUX, Introduction to Flip-Flops.
- Electronic Devices & Op-Amp Circuits: p-n junction diode, BJT operation, CB and CE configuration, Transistor as a switch. Ideal operational amplifier, inverting and non-inverting amplifiers, Op-amp as summing amplifier.
- Standards Familiarization: IEEE 1241-2023 (Analog-to-Digital), IEEE 1658-2011, IEEE 802 Family.
